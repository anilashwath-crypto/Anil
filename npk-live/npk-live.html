How to read this

Bands are general horticultural guidance for potting soil and garden beds, in mg/kg (ppm) of plant-available nutrient. Optimal ranges vary widely by crop — leafy greens want more nitrogen, fruiting plants more potassium.
Important accuracy caveat. Low-cost RS-485 NPK probes do not chemically measure N, P and K. They measure bulk electrical conductivity and derive all three numbers from that single signal through a fixed factory curve. The three values therefore move together and are indicative, not quantitative. Use them to track change over time in one pot, not as absolute soil chemistry. A lab soil test is the only way to get real N-P-K figures.

No reply from the probe

  1. Check the terminal screws first. Loose wires in the green block caused a total silence once already — no data, no error frames, no noise.
  2. Green → D+/A+ and White → D−/B−, per the probe's label (Green:RS485A, White:RS485B). A swapped pair is silent.
  3. Black → GND, Red → 5V. Supply spec is 5–30 VDC so the adapter's 5 V rail is fine.
